ELY Shop Review

An audit of shop.elywinebar.ie on Shopify, a feasibility check for AI bottle images, and what a prototype can show the owner.

Shop concept · Listing tool

In short
  1. The range and the writing are strong. The shop does not show them well.
  2. The image problem is bigger than inconsistency. 28 of 92 wine listings show another wine's photo or a different vintage.
  3. Most photos do not need AI. A simple script puts 87 of 92 on one studio standard. Only 5 need a reshoot or an AI studio shot.
  4. The main cause of the "faff" is the filters. Every filter is a collection kept by hand, and 86 of the 179 filter links open an empty page.
  5. The real win is a one-photo "add a bottle" flow into Shopify, with filters built from wine data. It fixes images, data and the slow listing rate together.

What works well

  • A specialist range. About 90 wines over 8 pages. Deep Italian and Spanish lists: Arpepe verticals back to 2013, Le Piane, Finca Sandoval, Mas Alta, Alta Alella.
  • Good filters already exist. Type, country, grape (60+), style (organic, natural, biodynamic, vegan) and vintage.
  • Descriptions by people who know the wine. Vine age, maceration days, soil, sub-zone, food.
  • The shop connects to the bars. Tastings, winemaker dinners, Bottle & Bites, vouchers, gifting, click and collect in Maynooth.
  • Clear delivery terms. €15 nationwide, 2–5 days.

What does not work

High

Images show the wrong vintage or the wrong wine

28 of 92 listings. Buon Consiglio 2016 and both Sant Antonio vintages show an Inferno Sesto Canto 2009 label. Ultimi Raggi and all three Rocce Rosse listings share one photo with a 2009 label. Stella Retica shares the Fiamme Antiche photo. Salia and La Rosa have swapped photos. The Telmo Rodriguez 2020 wines show 2017 labels. Il Pettirosso 2023 shows 2017. A buyer of a €129 bottle notices. It is also a consumer-law risk.

High

Filters are 179 hand-kept collections, and 86 are empty

Each grape, country, region and vintage filter is its own collection. Adding one bottle means adding it to five or more collections by hand. 86 links open an empty page: Australia, Germany, Riesling, Chablis, 2014 and others. 11 wines are in no Red / White / Sparkling collection, so those filters miss them.

Medium

The theme is from 2021 and pre-dates Online Store 2.0

The theme is "Luxywine" 1.0, a third-party theme. It cannot show metafields or use Search & Discovery filters without code changes. Budget for theme work, or move to a current Shopify theme.

High

Homepage shows placeholder products

"What we are drinking" shows 12 cards titled "Example Title" at $160 → $80, in dollars. The theme section has no collection set. The heading above it reads "Reccomendations". This is a five-minute fix and the most visible problem on the site.

Medium

Four image styles in one grid

Supplier packshots at 400×400 px (soft on phones). Cut-outs from 2021 at 800×800 px with rough edges. Phone photos on grey paper at 4284×5712 px, portrait, so the grid crops them differently. A few WebP files. Bottle size and position change from tile to tile.

Medium

Wine facts are missing or live only in prose

No alcohol, bottle size, drinking window or serving temperature. Grapes and region sit in the description text, so they cannot drive filters or comparisons. The Reviews tab is empty.

Medium

Filters drift from the stock

The vintage filter stops at 2021. The shop sells 2022, 2023 and 2024 wines. The filters run on hand-typed tags, and nobody updates them.

Low

Titles follow no pattern

"ArPePe" and "Arpepe". Some titles have the vintage, some do not (Finca Sandoval La Rosa, Mas Alta Artigas). Some add the appellation, some do not. Rocce Rosse 2016 is listed twice. One Zarate listing gives its vintage as "20121". Two Le Piane descriptions open with "For multiorders please contact us here." The web address ending "le-piane-boca-2018" opens La Maggiorina 2022. Seven addresses name a different vintage, and 19 still contain "copy" from duplicated listings.

Low

Wine is mixed with deli and gifts

"All products" runs to 15 pages: pasta, mustard, candles, greeting cards. A €3.75 item named "Gift Card" is probably a greeting card, not a voucher.

AI bottle images: feasibility

Yes, it is feasible. Cost per image is small. Label accuracy is the one hard problem. In your app a changed label is a small flaw. In a shop the image is a promise about the product, so the bar is higher. The scrape also shows that most of the catalogue does not need AI at all.

A. Shopify Magic, in the admin

Free for now

Built into the Shopify admin (Content → Files → edit). It removes a background and sets a solid colour, changes the scene from a text prompt, or extends the frame. One image at a time, about 1 megapixel, with an invisible watermark. Use it for the few gift and lifestyle shots. It does not put 92 bottles at one size and position.

Shopify Help: media generation in the file editor

B. Cut-out and composite, no AI

Built · 87 of 92

A script finds the bottle on a white or transparent source, crops it, scales it to 84% of the frame and places it on one backdrop with one shadow. The label pixels do not change, so there is no text risk. It handled 87 of the 92 wine photos. The prototype shows the result as "Studio standard".

C. Nano Banana relight, with a check

For phone photos

For the 5 photos on busy backgrounds, and for every new bottle staff photograph. Send the phone photo and one reference studio shot to Nano Banana 2. It returns a relit, straightened studio shot. Then a second model reads the label on the output and compares producer, wine and vintage with the listing. A mismatch goes to a person.

Nano Banana 2 (gemini-3.1-flash-image-preview): about $0.045–0.15 per image, half price in batch.

Do not

Generate a bottle from a label scan or a description alone. The model guesses glass colour, bottle shape and capsule. That is a picture of a wine you do not sell.

Note for your app

The original Nano Banana (gemini-2.5-flash-image) stops on the Gemini API on 2 October 2026 (Vertex AI: March 2027). Move your app to Nano Banana 2 before then. It renders label text better and is faster.

The listing bottleneck

Images are one part of the "faff". Typing facts, tags and descriptions is likely the larger part. The prototype shows one flow for all of it:

  1. Staff take one phone photo of the bottle.
  2. A vision model reads the label: producer, wine, vintage, region, grape, ABV, size. It marks fields it is unsure of.
  3. Nano Banana makes the studio shot. The check compares it with the label.
  4. The tool saves a Draft product in Shopify with metafields, image and a first description.
  5. A person sets the price and approves.

Shopify has no built-in feature that reads a label and creates a product. Sidekick writes text and edits images, but does not do this. So the build is one small app for this store, made in Shopify's Dev Dashboard (since January 2026, new custom apps cannot be made in the store admin).

Marketing: Erik's Wine of the Week

In the shop, Erik explains, recommends and helps people choose. Online, nobody does that. A short weekly series brings the shop conversation to the people who never come in. Farrow & Ball does the same with Patrick O'Donnell: one expert, one voice, advice people come back for.

Gaps in the plan

Erik's time. The series needs about one hour a week from Erik, plus someone to film, cut and post. It stops the first week that has no owner. Decide who owns it before the first episode.

Stock depth. A pick that sells out on day two frustrates people. Pick wines with at least a case in stock.

Alcohol advertising rules. The ASAI code applies now: nothing aimed at under-18s, no link to social or sexual success, and Meta audiences set to 18+. Section 13 of the Public Health (Alcohol) Act has not yet commenced. If it does, it limits ads to factual content with health warnings. A factual tasting-note format fits that better than lifestyle content. Get advice before paid promotion.

Check the current social accounts first. See what ELY posts now, how often, and what gets a response, before proposing the series.

The problem may not be images. The owner named images. Wrong labels and hand-kept filters cost more. Ask him to time one new listing today, including the collections. That number sets the value of the tool.

Someone must own the tool. A custom app needs API keys, hosting and fixes when models change. Decide who pays for and maintains it before you build.

Staff must take the photo. The tool needs a real photo of each bottle. For wines not yet in stock, supplier shots still arrive. Decide if those go through option B or wait for the bottle.

Your app and his shop have different stakes. A small label change in your app is cosmetic. In his shop it misdescribes a product he sells.
