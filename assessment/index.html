ELY Shop Review

An audit of shop.elywinebar.ie on Shopify, a feasibility check for AI bottle images, and what a prototype can show the owner.

Open the prototype

In short
  1. The range and the writing are strong. The shop does not show them well.
  2. The image problem is bigger than inconsistency. At least five listings show the wrong vintage or the wrong wine.
  3. AI studio shots are feasible and cheap (a few cents per image). The risk is that the model changes label text. A check step solves it.
  4. The real win is a one-photo "add a bottle" flow. It fixes images, data and the slow listing rate together.

What works well

  • A specialist range. About 90 wines over 8 pages. Deep Italian and Spanish lists: Arpepe verticals back to 2013, Le Piane, Finca Sandoval, Mas Alta, Alta Alella.
  • Good filters already exist. Type, country, grape (60+), style (organic, natural, biodynamic, vegan) and vintage.
  • Descriptions by people who know the wine. Vine age, maceration days, soil, sub-zone, food.
  • The shop connects to the bars. Tastings, winemaker dinners, Bottle & Bites, vouchers, gifting, click and collect in Maynooth.
  • Clear delivery terms. €15 nationwide, 2–5 days.

What does not work

High

Images show the wrong vintage or the wrong wine

Buon Consiglio 2016 (Grumello) shows an Inferno Sesto Canto 2009 label. Rocce Rosse 2018 shows a 2009 label. Il Pettirosso 2023 shows a 2017 label. Nuova Regina 2013, 2016 and 2018 share one image. Both Fiamme Antiche vintages share one image. A buyer of a €129 bottle notices. It is also a consumer-law risk.

High

Homepage shows placeholder products

"What we are drinking" shows 12 cards titled "Example Title" at $160 → $80, in dollars. The theme section has no collection set. The heading above it reads "Reccomendations". This is a five-minute fix and the most visible problem on the site.

Medium

Four image styles in one grid

Supplier packshots at 400×400 px (soft on phones). Cut-outs from 2021 at 800×800 px with rough edges. Phone photos on grey paper at 4284×5712 px, portrait, so the grid crops them differently. A few WebP files. Bottle size and position change from tile to tile.

Medium

Wine facts are missing or live only in prose

No alcohol, bottle size, drinking window or serving temperature. Grapes and region sit in the description text, so they cannot drive filters or comparisons. The Reviews tab is empty.

Medium

Filters drift from the stock

The vintage filter stops at 2021. The shop sells 2022, 2023 and 2024 wines. The filters run on hand-typed tags, and nobody updates them.

Low

Titles follow no pattern

"ArPePe" and "Arpepe". Some titles have the vintage, some do not (Finca Sandoval La Rosa, Mas Alta Artigas). Some add the appellation, some do not.

Low

Wine is mixed with deli and gifts

"All products" runs to 15 pages: pasta, mustard, candles, greeting cards. A €3.75 item named "Gift Card" is probably a greeting card, not a voucher.

AI bottle images: feasibility

Yes, it is feasible. Cost per image is small. Label accuracy is the one hard problem. In your app a changed label is a small flaw. In a shop the image is a promise about the product, so the bar is higher.

A. Shopify Magic, in the admin

Free

Removes the background and sets a flat colour or a generated scene. One image at a time. It does not fix angle, glare or light. Good for a quick clean-up this week.

B. Cut-out and composite

~€0.02–0.20 per image

Background removal API, then place the bottle on a fixed backdrop with a fixed shadow. The label pixels do not change, so there is no text risk. The result is only as good as the phone photo.

C. Nano Banana relight, with a check

Recommended

Send the phone photo and one reference studio shot to Nano Banana 2 or Nano Banana Pro. It returns a relit, straightened studio shot. Then a second model reads the label on the output and compares producer, wine and vintage with the listing. A mismatch goes to a person. If C fails twice, fall back to B.

Nano Banana 2: about $0.045–0.15 per image, half price in batch. 100 wines at three tries each is about €15–45.

Do not

Generate a bottle from a label scan or a description alone. The model guesses glass colour, bottle shape and capsule. That is a picture of a wine you do not sell.

Note for your app

The original Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) retires on 2 October 2026. Move your app to Nano Banana 2 before then.

The listing bottleneck

Images are one part of the "faff". Typing facts, tags and descriptions is likely the larger part. The prototype shows one flow for all of it:

  1. Staff take one phone photo of the bottle.
  2. A vision model reads the label: producer, wine, vintage, region, grape, ABV, size. It marks fields it is unsure of.
  3. Nano Banana makes the studio shot. The check compares it with the label.
  4. The tool saves a Draft product in Shopify with metafields, image and a first description.
  5. A person sets the price and approves.

Build: a small Shopify custom app (Admin API: product create, staged uploads, metafields) with a phone-friendly page. About 1–2 weeks for a first version. Running cost: a few euro a month plus hosting.

Gaps in the plan

The problem may not be images. The owner named images. Wrong labels and slow listing cost more. Ask him to time one new listing today. That number sets the value of the tool.

Someone must own the tool. A custom app needs API keys, hosting and fixes when models change. Decide who pays for and maintains it before you build.

Staff must take the photo. The tool needs a real photo of each bottle. For wines not yet in stock, supplier shots still arrive. Decide if those go through option B or wait for the bottle.

Your app and his shop have different stakes. A small label change in your app is cosmetic. In his shop it misdescribes a product he sells.

What the prototype shows

  • Shop: one studio standard across the grid, with style, region, grape and price on each card.
  • Wine page: Le Piane Boca 2018 with facts, taste scale, drinking window, food, a "pouring at the bar" link, and more from the producer. A toggle shows the phone photo it came from.
  • Add a bottle: the four-step flow, run on Rocce Rosse 2018, the wine that shows a 2009 label today.

The bottles in the prototype are drawn in code, not generated. A real version uses a Nano Banana output from a photo of each bottle. Taste values and drinking windows are examples for the team to set.
